The problem

You have bought this before. Nobody filled it in.

Every CRM sold to a distributor dies the same way. It arrives empty. It stays empty, because it depends on salespeople typing up calls they have already made, from a vehicle, at the end of a long day. Three months later you are paying for a database of nothing.

A CRM knows only what was typed

And the one thing a field sales team will never do reliably is type. So the system is wrong from the first week, and being wrong is worse than being empty.

An ERP knows everything and says nothing

Your accounts package holds the stock, the costs, the expiry dates and every invoice. It has never once told a salesman which customer to ring this morning.

So customers leave quietly

Nobody decides to stop buying. They just order less, then not at all. Without something watching, you find out a year later when you look at the annual figures.

How it works

It starts working before anyone logs in

Invoices get entered whether or not your team likes software, because that is how you get paid. That makes them the only complete record in the business, and it is enough.

1

Bring in your history

Export customers, products and past invoices from whatever you use now. Beatplan reads the column headings, shows you what it understood, and lets you correct it before anything is saved.

2

It learns each customer's rhythm

Not one rule for everybody. A bakery ordering every 12 days and a hotel ordering quarterly are both normal. Beatplan measures each customer against their own pattern.

3

Your team gets a list, every morning

In order, with the reason next to each name and what that customer usually buys. Sent by email before the day starts and waiting in the app, online or not.

What makes it different

It is the only one that can say no.

A CRM will happily send your salesman to push a product that is out of stock, three weeks from expiry, or priced below what you paid for it, because it has no idea what is in your warehouse. Beatplan holds both halves, so it can refuse.

Never sell what you cannot supply

Anything the reorder sheet flags as short is barred from every push list. Winning an order you cannot fill costs more than the order was worth.

Oldest stock moves first

Push lists name the batch, not just the product, so the pallet that expires in March goes before the one that arrived last week.

An alarm when price drops below cost

A packet price entered against a bale, a stale cost after a currency move. It surfaces the moment it happens instead of at the year end.

Reserved stock is not sellable

Goods already invoiced and waiting for collection are held, so the same six units cannot be promised to two customers.

Discounts stay the owner's decision. Beatplan never invents one.

Questions

The ones everybody asks

Do I have to replace my accounts package?

No, and you should not. Sage, QuickBooks, Odoo or whatever you run stays exactly where it is and remains the record of truth for invoicing and stock. Beatplan reads from it and tells your sales team what to do. It never writes back.

How long before it is useful?

As long as it takes to export three spreadsheets. Once your customers and invoice history are in, the call list is there the same day, because it is calculated from history rather than built up from new activity.

My salesmen will not use new software.

That is the correct thing to worry about, and it is why the main screen is a list with names and reasons rather than a dashboard or a search box. There is nothing to fill in before it works. If your team logs nothing at all, it still tells you who has gone quiet, because that comes from the invoices.

What happens where there is no mobile signal?

Everything keeps working. The list is on the phone, calls and orders are saved on the phone, and it all syncs when a connection comes back. It was built for people who spend the day in a vehicle.

Is my data separated from other companies?

Yes, and it is enforced by the database itself rather than by application code, with an automated test suite that tries to break the separation on every change. Each company can also be given its own web address.

Can we pay in something other than dollars?

Card payments are billed in US dollars by our Estonian company. In Zambia we invoice locally and accept mobile money. For anywhere else, ask.
